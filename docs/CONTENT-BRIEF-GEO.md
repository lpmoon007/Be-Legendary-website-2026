## The key finding that shapes this brief

Every target page **already has** FAQPage schema and a short-answer ("speakable") block.
Your 25-citation winner (`/why-isnt-my-executive-team-executing/`) and the pages where
you get **zero** citations are built the same way. So structure is not the lever.

What separates the winner is that it gives a **distinctive, enumerated, attributable
answer** in the buyer's exact words: *"it's rarely strategy or effort — it's discipline…
here are the four ways execution breaks."* AI engines lift answers that are specific,
opinionated, named, and listable. McKinsey and HBR win the pages you lose because they
have brand authority on **generic** phrasing; you beat them only by being **sharper and
more specific** than they are willing to be, and by owning the phrasing they genericize.

## The citeable-answer standard (every target page must hit all 7)

1. **First 40–60 words answer the literal prompt** with a committed, slightly contrarian
   claim — not a hedge. (The winner does this; McKinsey hedges.)
2. **A named, numbered structure** in the answer (the four disciplines; the 4Ds — Owner,
   Data, Deadline, Defend). Lists and named frameworks get lifted verbatim.
3. **A stance the big brands won't take.** You can say "coaching the individuals won't fix
   a team-level discipline problem." Specificity is your moat, not authority.
4. **Match the prompt's exact nouns.** "executive team" and "leadership team" are different
   strings to a model; the H1 + one FAQ question should carry the literal prompt wording.
5. **An FAQ question worded as the prompt itself**, answered in 2–4 sentences at the top of
   that answer.
6. **Fresh `dateModified`** on every edit (recency is a ranking/citation signal).
7. **Internal links from the winner** (`/why-isnt-my-executive-team-executing/`, 25 cites)
   **to** the weak/absent pages — co-citation and authority flow from your strongest node.
