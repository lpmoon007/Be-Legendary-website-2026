# Commitment Design Spec — deriving three lead-measure commitments from transcripts

**Audience:** a project (or a person) that reads someone's **transcripts,
conversations, and topics** and produces **three commitments** for the 30-Day
Challenge. It defines what a good commitment is, the exact format to produce, the
habit science behind it, how to mine source material, a quality bar, and an output
schema.

> **The single most important rule (read first):** the trigger — the "When I ___"
> — must be an **existing daily habit the person already does at a reliable time**
> (an *anchor*). You are **stacking** the new behavior onto an old one. A trigger
> that fires on an *event* ("when I see an opportunity," "when something goes well,"
> "when I'm about to decide") is **opportunistic** — the person has to catch the
> moment, and they won't. Opportunistic triggers are the #1 reason a well-intentioned
> commitment quietly fails. Anchor every commitment to a habit that already happens.

---

## 1. What a "commitment" is here

A commitment is **one daily lead measure** — the smallest behavior a person can
practice every day that, repeated, produces the change they're after.

In the 30-Day Challenge it becomes their **daily action**: a reminder fires *at the
anchor moment*, they do the small behavior, an afternoon check-in asks them to rate
the effort 1–10, and a one-line reflection asks how it went. So every commitment
must be a **single, specific, controllable action that rides an existing daily
habit and can be rated on an ordinary day.**

- **Lag measure** = the outcome ("be more trusted"). You can't *do* it.
- **Lead measure** = the controllable daily behavior that drives it. This is the goal.
- **Opportunistic measure** = a behavior with no reliable trigger ("help when I
  can"). Looks like a lead measure but has no anchor, so it doesn't get done.

---

## 2. The commitment format

> **When I \_\_\_, instead of \_\_\_, I will \_\_\_.**
>
> *When I [existing daily habit — the anchor], instead of [the default I do at that
> moment], I will [small new action].*

| Slot | Name | Job |
|------|------|-----|
| **When I \_\_\_** | the **anchor** | An existing habit you already do at a reliable time. The new behavior stacks on it. |
| **instead of \_\_\_** | the **default** | The automatic thing you do at that anchor moment — named honestly. |
| **I will \_\_\_** | the **action** (lead measure) | The small, specific new behavior you'll do at that anchor instead. |

**Worked shape (the canonical example):**

> When I **brush my teeth in the morning**, instead of just standing at the mirror,
> I will **do one squat**.

The reminder lands at ~6:02, the anchor (brushing) is at 6:03, and the new action
rides on top. That pairing — reminder + existing habit — is what makes it happen.

---

## 3. Why this works — the habit science

**Habit-stacking / anchoring (BJ Fogg, *Tiny Habits*; James Clear, *Atomic Habits*).**
The reliable cue of an existing habit becomes the cue for the new one ("after I
[current habit], I will [new habit]"). This is the mechanism that makes an intention
actually fire. **It is the core of this whole method.**

**Implementation intentions / if-then plans (Gollwitzer).** Pre-deciding "when
situation X, I will do Y" raises follow-through 2–3×. Anchoring supplies a *reliable*
X so the plan triggers every day instead of occasionally.

**Lead measures (4DX / McChesney).** Act on the controllable behavior, not the
lagging outcome. The "I will" is the lead measure.

**Competing response (habit-reversal research).** You can't erase a routine, only
replace it while keeping the cue. The "instead of… I will…" pair is that swap.

**Small = done (Fogg; Clear).** The smaller the action, the higher the completion
rate. Consistency beats intensity.

**Repetition to automaticity (Lally et al.).** Behaviors automate through repeated
pairing of a **frequent** cue with the same response. A daily anchor supplies the reps.

## 4. The quality bar for each slot

### When I \_\_\_ (the anchor) — the make-or-break slot
- **It must be an existing habit the person already does** — pouring coffee,
  opening their laptop, the first email, lunch, closing their laptop, brushing
  teeth, the commute. If they don't already do it reliably, it is not an anchor.
- **It happens at a predictable time**, so a reminder can land on it and so the
  action rides an automatic cue.
- **It's observable and catchable in the moment** — the person can notice it
  starting.
- **NOT an event they must catch** — "when I see an opportunity," "when something
  goes well," "when a conflict arises," "when I'm stressed." These have no reliable
  time, so the reminder can't anchor and the behavior gets missed.

**Anchored (good) vs opportunistic (bad):**

| Anchored ✅ | Opportunistic ❌ |
|---|---|
| When I pour my first coffee… | When I see an opportunity to help… |
| When I open my laptop to start work… | When something goes well… |
| When I sit down for lunch… | When I'm about to make a decision… |
| When I close my laptop at the end of the day… | When a hard conversation comes up… |

### instead of \_\_\_ (the default)
- Names the **real, specific** thing they currently do at that anchor (scrolling
  their phone, diving into their own list, just clicking off). Behavioral, honest —
  they should nod "yes, that's what I do."

### I will \_\_\_ (the action)
- A **behavior, not an outcome** ("ask one question," not "make them feel heard").
- **Small enough for a bad day** — one message, one question, one squat.
- **Fully in their control** — depends on no one else's response.
- **Approach-framed** — says what to *do*, not only what to stop.
- **Ratable 1–10 daily** — "did I do it, and how well?"

## 5. Method — from transcript to three commitments

### Step A — Mine the source for patterns
Look for self-described tendencies ("I always/tend to/keep…"), recurring friction,
feedback from others, charged moments, values–behavior gaps, and repeated topics.
Capture the exact quote.

### Step B — For each theme, name the change
What's the small approach behavior that would move it?

### Step C — Find the anchor (do this deliberately — it's where most drafts fail)
Ask: **what does this person already do every day, at a reliable time, near the
moment this behavior matters?** That existing habit is the "When I." If you can't
find a real anchor, the commitment isn't ready — reshape it around one. Then name
the default they do at that anchor, and the small action that replaces it.

### Step D — Select the three
High-frequency anchors, high-leverage behaviors, controllable, distinct from each
other (ideally different anchors so they don't collide). Rank by leverage.

### Step E — Pressure-test against §6.

## 6. Quality checklist (apply to every commitment)

- [ ] **The trigger is an existing daily habit (an anchor), NOT an opportunistic event**
- [ ] The anchor happens at a **predictable time** a reminder can land on
- [ ] "Instead of" names the **real default** they do at that anchor moment
- [ ] "I will" is a **behavior, not an outcome**
- [ ] "I will" is **small enough to do on a bad day**
- [ ] "I will" is **fully in their control**
- [ ] **Approach-framed** (do X, not only stop Y)
- [ ] **Ratable 1–10 daily**
- [ ] Uses the **person's own language / context**
- [ ] **Distinct** from the other two (different anchors where possible)

---

## 7. Why three — and how they relate to the challenge

The 30-Day Challenge runs **one action at a time**. Treat the three as a **ranked
shortlist**: lead with the highest-leverage one for the first 30 days; the other two
are the next cycles or alternatives. If any run in parallel, make sure their anchors
fire at **different moments** so the actions don't collide. Mark which to lead with.

## 8. Worked examples (source signal → anchored commitment)

**1 · Recognition**
Signal: *"I never tell people they did a good job — I just move on."*
→ *When I pour my first coffee, instead of scrolling my phone, I will send one
message telling someone exactly what they did that mattered.*

**2 · Reactivity**
Signal: *"I fire back on email within two minutes when something annoys me."*
→ *When I open my inbox, instead of replying to the thing that's bugging me first, I
will draft it, leave it, and send only after I've reread it once.*

**3 · Developing people**
Signal: *"Whenever the team hits a snag I just take it over and fix it."*
→ *When I sit down at my desk to start the day, instead of diving into my own list,
I will hand one thing to someone on my team — a decision, a task, or a question
instead of my answer.*

**4 · Conflict avoidance**
Signal: *"I let things slide rather than have the hard conversation."*
→ *When I pour my first coffee, instead of easing into the day, I will take one small
step toward the conversation I've been putting off.*

**5 · Awareness**
Signal: *"I run on autopilot and miss what's right in front of me."*
→ *When I sit down for lunch, instead of reaching for my phone, I will look up and
name one person or one thing I'd otherwise walk past.*

## 9. Anti-patterns and fixes

| Anti-pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunistic trigger** | "When I see a chance to help…" | Anchor to a habit: "When I open my laptop, … I will do one thing that helps someone first." |
| No real anchor | "When I remember to…" | Tie it to something they already do daily. |
| Outcome, not behavior | "…I will be a better listener." | Name the observable act. |
| Action too big | "…I will rebuild trust." | Shrink to one in-the-moment action. |
| Not controllable | "…they will feel heard." | Restate as your action. |
| Only negative | "…I will stop interrupting." | Add the approach behavior to do instead. |
| Strawman default | "instead of being terrible at this" | Name the real thing they do at the anchor. |

## 10. Output schema (what the project should return)

```json
{
  "commitment": "When I ___, instead of ___, I will ___.",
  "anchor": "the WHEN — the existing daily habit it stacks on",
  "default_reaction": "the INSTEAD OF — what they currently do at that anchor",
  "action": "the I WILL — the small new behavior (the daily lead measure)",
  "anchor_is_existing_habit": true,
  "derived_from": "exact quote or pattern from the source material",
  "anchor_time_of_day": "morning | midday | end of day | variable",
  "domain": "e.g., recognition, delegation, conflict, self-regulation, awareness",
  "why_it_matters": "one line tying it to their stated goal or values",
  "lead_with": true
}
```

Return **three distinct** entries, each with `anchor_is_existing_habit: true`,
ranked by leverage, exactly one marked `lead_with: true`. If a candidate can't be
anchored to a real existing habit, **don't ship it** — reshape it until it can.

---

## 11. One-line summary

Stack a small new **action** onto a habit the person **already does** — *When I
[existing daily habit], instead of [my default there], I will [small action]* —
never on an opportunistic event they'd have to catch. Anchor first; everything else
is secondary.
